# SWOT Analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats)

## What it is
SWOT analysis provides a structured snapshot of an organization's internal position (Strengths, Weaknesses) relative to its external environment (Opportunities, Threats). In consulting proposals, SWOT is rarely a deliverable in itself — it is a diagnostic input that grounds the problem statement and informs hypothesis prioritization. It answers: "Why is this client uniquely positioned to act on this problem, and what will make success harder?"

## When to use it
- The engagement involves strategic repositioning, market entry, or competitive response
- The client needs to understand why now is the right time to act (urgency creation)
- The problem statement requires grounding in both internal capabilities and external dynamics
- The client's leadership team has divergent views on the company's strengths — alignment is needed before recommendations

## Consulting language
"As part of the base information set, we will develop a SWOT analysis grounded in expert interviews and industry benchmarking…", "The SWOT will provide the strategic context for our hypothesis prioritization, ensuring that improvement measures are calibrated to the client's actual capabilities and constraints…", "We will conduct structured workshops with [N] senior stakeholders to develop a fact-based assessment of [Company]'s competitive position."

## Customization guidance
- Never present a generic 2×2 box. Label each quadrant with the client's actual business context
- Prioritize: not all SWOTs are equal. Rank the top 2–3 most decision-relevant items in each quadrant
- In efficiency-focused engagements: rename to "Capability Assessment" (Strengths → "Existing levers", Weaknesses → "Efficiency gaps")
- In market-entry engagements: weight Opportunities and Threats more heavily; compress the internal quadrants
- Always connect SWOT findings directly to the hypotheses: "The identified weakness in [area] gives rise to Hypothesis 3…"
- Gather external quadrants from market research and industry benchmarks; gather internal quadrants from expert interviews or workshops

## Example deliverables in a proposal
- "A SWOT analysis based on [N] expert interviews and industry benchmark data, identifying the top strategic priorities for [Company]"
- "A capability assessment mapping [Company]'s strengths against the requirements of [strategic objective]"
- "A structured stakeholder workshop to develop a shared, fact-based view of [Company]'s competitive position"
