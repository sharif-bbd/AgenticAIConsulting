# Minto Pyramid Principle (Executive Communication Structure)

## What it is
The Minto Pyramid Principle, developed by Barbara Minto at McKinsey, is the standard structure for executive communication in consulting. It follows SCQA (Situation → Complication → Question → Answer) and presents conclusions first, followed by supporting arguments in a pyramid hierarchy. It is how senior consultants write proposals, presentations, and recommendations that busy executives can absorb in 60 seconds.

**SCQA structure:**
- **Situation**: The current, agreed-upon state of affairs (what the client already knows)
- **Complication**: The change or problem that disrupts the situation (the burning platform)
- **Question**: The question this complication raises (what needs to be resolved)
- **Answer**: The recommended response (the proposal)

## When to use it
- As the structural principle for ALL sections of the proposal (it is not just a framework — it is the writing discipline)
- To draft the "Understanding of the Situation" section: Situation + Complication = why we are here
- To structure the introduction / cover letter of the proposal
- When the client needs to be guided from problem-awareness to solution-acceptance efficiently

## Consulting language
This framework is not referenced by name in proposals — it IS the proposal structure. Use it to structure every paragraph: start with the conclusion (the "so what"), then provide the support.

**Wrong (bottom-up):** "We analyzed cost data, reviewed industry benchmarks, and interviewed 12 stakeholders. We found that RtB costs grew 18% last year. Therefore, an efficiency program is needed."

**Right (pyramid):** "An efficiency program is needed urgently: RtB costs grew 18% last year against an industry benchmark of 3%, driven by [specific causes]. Our analysis confirms this trajectory is unsustainable within 6 months."

## Customization guidance
- The SCQA structure should map directly to the "Understanding of the Situation" section:
  - Situation = "Over the past [N] months, you have been [working on / facing / experiencing]…"
  - Complication = "However, [problem / pressure / gap]…"
  - Question = "This raises the central question: [how / whether / what]…"
  - Answer = "This proposal outlines how we will work together to answer that question."
- Ensure the Complication explicitly states the cost of inaction (urgency)
- The Question should be the exact "Question to be Answered" that runs through the entire proposal

## Example deliverables in a proposal
- Applied as a structural principle — not a named deliverable
- "A structured executive presentation following the Situation-Complication-Question-Answer framework, ensuring rapid absorption by senior leadership"
