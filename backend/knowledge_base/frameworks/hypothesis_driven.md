# Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving

## What it is
Hypothesis-driven problem solving is the core methodology of top-tier management consulting. Rather than gathering all available data before drawing conclusions, the consultant formulates explicit hypotheses at the outset — educated guesses about what the answer likely is — and then designs the minimum set of analyses needed to prove or disprove each hypothesis. This approach delivers 80% of the insight in 20% of the time by focusing effort on the questions that matter most.

The methodology has three phases: (1) Hypothesize — articulate what you believe to be true and why; (2) Analyze — design targeted analyses to test each hypothesis; (3) Synthesize — draw conclusions and build recommendations from confirmed/rejected hypotheses.

## When to use it
- The engagement is time-boxed (fixed effort, fixed fee)
- The client organization lacks the analytical bandwidth for exhaustive studies
- Political dynamics require early stakeholder alignment around a directional answer
- The problem space is well-understood enough to make informed initial guesses

## Consulting language
"Our methodology will follow a hypothesis-driven approach, formulating and prioritizing a set of key propositions before designing targeted analyses…", "We will develop an extended list of hypotheses, specifying which facts are needed to validate each one and what analyses would be required…", "The hypotheses will be continuously refined as we gather evidence, ensuring that our analytical effort is always directed at the most decision-relevant questions."

## Customization guidance
- Always link hypotheses explicitly to the client's stated problem and strategic context
- Distinguish between "structural hypotheses" (about what is causing the problem) and "solution hypotheses" (about what would fix it)
- In proposals, show the hypothesis list as a table: Hypothesis | Evidence required | Analysis type | Priority
- Pair with an issue tree: the issue tree gives structure, hypothesis-driven work gives speed

## Example deliverables in a proposal
- "A prioritized hypothesis register with evidence requirements and analysis specifications"
- "A focused analytical workplan derived from hypothesis prioritization, covering [N] high-priority areas"
- "Interim hypothesis status updates at each steering committee meeting, allowing the team to redirect effort as evidence accumulates"
